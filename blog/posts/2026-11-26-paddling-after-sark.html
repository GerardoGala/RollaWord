Paddling After dark

Lucas Timothy November 26, 2026
Coastline
  • We brought out two different setups for the session: a bright orange two-person canoe and a stand-up paddleboard. Taking both out at the same time always makes for a fun dynamic. In the canoe, it is all about finding a steady, shared rhythm matching strokes from the front and back so the boat tracks straight across the bay. Meanwhile, standing up on the paddleboard gives you a completely different vantage point, letting you look straight down into the clear shallows while balancing with the slight swell of the tide

  • You don't always need a big itinerary or rough waves to make a trip to the coast worthwhile. Sometimes, grabbing a paddle, heading out with good company, and staying out just a little past sunset is the best way to wrap up the day.

  • Staying out on the water as daylight fades into dusk changes the whole feel of a normal paddle. Without the glare of the midday sun, everything slows down. You pay a little more attention to the sound of the paddles dipping into the water, the faint outline of the distant hills, and the gradual shift from twilight to evening.
